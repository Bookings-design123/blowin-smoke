## Executive Finding

This is directly relevant to Blowin' Smoke's disposable-vape research.

> A familiar candy-forward lead flavor can work extremely well when sweetness is controlled, the secondary fruit remains detectable, and the total combination feels intentional.

The reviewer strongly likes the product. His recurring criticism is equally useful: **dragon fruit is too weak relative to strawberry.** He estimates roughly **65% strawberry / 35% dragon fruit** and wants more dragon fruit.

The strongest behavioral endorsement is not the 88.8 score. It is his statement that the device will be **in his pocket and frequently in his hand**. That is a stronger anticipated-use signal than generic praise.

## Customer Priorities Revealed

### 1. Multi-note flavor balance — Must-have
Both advertised notes are detectable, but strawberry dominates. A product can taste excellent while still under-delivering on its flavor-name promise.

**Repository rule:** score `overall_enjoyment` separately from `advertised_note_accuracy` and `flavor_balance`.

### 2. Candy character can be desirable — Segment-dependent delighter
Strawberry is compared positively to a strawberry fruit roll-up/candy. This directly contrasts with Entry 001's preference for natural, restrained dessert profiles.

**Implication:** Never create a universal rule that "natural tasting = better." Fruit-disposable buyers may actively prefer candy interpretation.

### 3. Sweetness has an optimum range — Must-have variable
The reviewer places sweetness around **5–6/10** and considers it well positioned, while later qualifying the recommendation with "if you can handle sweetness."

**Implication:** track sweetness explicitly rather than equating flavor intensity with sweetness.

### 4. Cooling must fit the flavor — Category-fit requirement
The transcript around ice is ambiguous, but the reviewer questions why ice would belong in Strawberry Dragon.

**Confidence:** medium-low. Do not assign a hard cooling score until corroborated.

### 5. Pleasant lingering finish — Delighter
He explicitly notices that the flavor lingers "in a good way."

**New field:** `finish_persistence`.

### 6. Portability can be traded for satisfaction — Tradeoff
He calls the device "a little bulky," yet still expects to carry/use it frequently.

**Implication:** size is not automatically a dealbreaker; measure whether performance compensates for bulk.

### 7. Reach behavior outranks adjectives — Major satisfaction signal
"Flawless" and "good" are weak evidence compared with the anticipated behavior: **frequently in his pocket/hand**.

**Methodological rule:** behavioral-use statements should carry more weight than generic adjectives or arbitrary scores.

## Must-Haves / Delighters / Tradeoffs / Potential Dealbreakers

**Must-haves**
- Advertised fruit notes remain detectable
- Sweetness stays tolerable
- Flavor combination feels intentional
- Product-name expectation matches actual flavor hierarchy

**Delighters**
- Pleasant lingering finish
- Strong aroma
- Candy strawberry for the appropriate segment
- Flavor strong enough to create frequent-reach behavior
- Distinctive fruit pairing

**Tradeoffs**
- Some device bulk if flavor compensates
- Secondary note weaker than lead if still detectable

**Potential dealbreakers to test**
- Excessive sweetness
- Secondary advertised flavor disappearing
- Cooling that conflicts with profile expectation
- Bulk without performance benefit
- Unpleasant lingering aftertaste
