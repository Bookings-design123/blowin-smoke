# 11. What Bay Smokes Does Well

## 11.1 Recognizes domain-specific proof

- **OBSERVED PRACTICE:** Selected product previews reference COAs, one exposes potency and physical/sensory attributes, and a lab-results route is prominent later.
- **CUSTOMER NEED SERVED:** Verify what the product contains and whether testing exists.
- **WHY IT WORKS:** It acknowledges that THCA trust requires evidence beyond generic quality language.
- **POTENTIAL BLOWIN' SMOKE LESSON:** Make proof status and current product/batch documentation a consistent part of the product system.

## 11.2 Uses recognizable format vocabulary

- **OBSERVED PRACTICE:** Flower, pre-rolls, concentrates, gummies, smalls, shake, snowballs, trim, bundles, and clubs appear in routes or names.
- **CUSTOMER NEED SERVED:** Enter the catalog using domain language.
- **WHY IT WORKS:** It reflects meaningful differences in form, value, and purchase pattern.
- **POTENTIAL BLOWIN' SMOKE LESSON:** Preserve customer vocabulary while separating product type, format, quantity/value, and collection in the underlying taxonomy.

## 11.3 Supports multiple discovery intentions

- **OBSERVED PRACTICE:** Customers can browse categories, favorites, top sellers, flower products, deals/bulk/bundles, or collaborations.
- **CUSTOMER NEED SERVED:** Serve known-category, exploratory, value-oriented, and affinity-led shoppers.
- **WHY IT WORKS:** It does not assume one path fits every level of category knowledge.
- **POTENTIAL BLOWIN' SMOKE LESSON:** Keep multiple routes but apply progressive disclosure and one dominant purpose per surface.

## 11.4 Addresses shipped-product anxiety

- **OBSERVED PRACTICE:** Shipping threshold, fast-shipping claims, discreet-packaging detail, legal explainer, age statement, and destination restrictions are present.
- **CUSTOMER NEED SERVED:** Understand privacy, delivery, and eligibility concerns.
- **WHY IT WORKS:** These are category-specific objections that ordinary retail trust modules miss.
- **POTENTIAL BLOWIN' SMOKE LESSON:** Surface concise, accurate eligibility and fulfillment facts at the decision point, with full policy one step away.

## 11.5 Educates inside merchandising

- **OBSERVED PRACTICE:** Rich previews explain smalls, strain, potency, appearance, scent, structure, and COA access.
- **CUSTOMER NEED SERVED:** Understand unfamiliar terms while considering a product.
- **WHY IT WORKS:** Education has immediate commercial relevance.
- **POTENTIAL BLOWIN' SMOKE LESSON:** Standardize concise format explanations and link to deeper, factual education.

## 11.6 Designs for repeat purchase

- **OBSERVED PRACTICE:** Monthly assortment, Flower Club, cash back, referral points, birthday rewards, and punchcard rewards are visible.
- **CUSTOMER NEED SERVED:** Replenish, save, and participate over time.
- **WHY IT WORKS:** Repeat purchase is treated as a designed lifecycle rather than an afterthought.
- **POTENTIAL BLOWIN' SMOKE LESSON:** Consider only a small number of programs with clear economics and real customer value after core product truth is solved.

## 11.7 Uses operator story as trust

- **CUSTOMER NEED SERVED:** Know who is behind the store and why their selection deserves attention.

# 12. What Bay Smokes Does Poorly

## 12.1 Mixed taxonomy

- **PROBLEM:** Product type, physical/value format, promotion, popularity, buying mode, and collaboration are presented as parallel routes.
- **CUSTOMER CONSEQUENCE:** It is difficult to know whether a label describes what the product is, how much it contains, why it is cheaper, or why the retailer is promoting it.
- **BLOWIN' SMOKE OPPORTUNITY:** Normalize product type, format, cannabinoid/composition, strain, quantity, brand, collection, and promotional state.

## 12.2 Inconsistent product-preview grammar

- **PROBLEM:** Lightweight cards expose name and price; rich cards add different subsets of proof, education, guarantees, and attributes.
- **CUSTOMER CONSEQUENCE:** Products cannot be compared with one learned scanning pattern.
- **BLOWIN' SMOKE OPPORTUNITY:** Use the Glossier-derived stable shell with THCA-specific fact slots and state-aware actions.

## 12.3 Product proof is selective

- **PROBLEM:** COA and potency appear on only some rich previews; general testing claims are not mapped to every product.
- **CUSTOMER CONSEQUENCE:** Customers cannot distinguish “proof unavailable,” “proof exists deeper,” and “proof applies to this exact item.”
