## 1. Authority and Purpose

This specification defines the original visual language that unifies Blowin' Smoke's house identity, three divisions, commerce states, education, proof, compatibility, and support. It governs relationships and roles rather than production values or implementation syntax.

Normative authority resolves in this order: `docs/constitution/01-brand-philosophy.md`; `docs/system/01-master-design-commerce-system.md`; `docs/system/02-information-architecture-page-system.md`; `docs/system/03-data-model-catalog-schema.md`; then `docs/system/04-page-by-page-architecture-specifications.md`. The logo is directly inspected brand evidence interpreted under that authority, not a higher-order policy source. The Glossier Design DNA supplies approved discipline—restraint, hierarchy, progressive disclosure, stable component grammar, state awareness, functional motion, and accessible control—but supplies no palette, typography, layout, card styling, imagery, effect, or trade dress.

The subordinate, closed design-discipline sources are `docs/research/glossier/01-homepage-final-design-dna.md`, `docs/research/glossier/02-product-page-final-design-dna.md`, and `docs/research/glossier/03-research-closure-design-system-decision.md`. They are inputs to principle transfer, not visual references to reproduce.

**ORIGINAL BLOWIN' SMOKE VISUAL DECISION:** The visual system is called **Pressure & Proof**. It concentrates pressure—confidence, graphic energy, cultural expression—at identity and editorial moments, then progressively removes visual noise as a customer approaches proof, fit, eligibility, configuration, cart, or support.

---

## 2. Central Governing Principle

> **THE CLOSER THE CUSTOMER GETS TO A CONSEQUENTIAL PURCHASE DECISION, THE LESS VISUAL NOISE THE INTERFACE SHOULD INTRODUCE.**

This is not a split personality or a 50/50 mashup. The commerce foundation inherits more heavily from the approved Glossier-derived discipline: spacious structure, low chrome, predictable components, precise hierarchy, literal states, and progressive disclosure. Blowin' Smoke expression enters where it creates leverage rather than interference.

| BRAND ENERGY BELONGS | QUIET PRECISION BELONGS |
|---|---|
| House identity and logo moments | Product configuration |
| Homepage editorial composition | Proof and COA interpretation |
| Division entry and curated edits | Electronic compatibility and physical fit |
| Select display typography | Eligibility and technical specifications |
| Original imagery/art direction | Cart validation and support escalation |
| Graphic framing and section transitions | Errors, unknowns, stale and missing data |
| Direct, culturally credible copy | Any state whose misreading can create an unusable or restricted purchase |

Energy establishes memory. Restraint earns trust. Neither substitutes for product truth.

## 3. Direct Logo Inspection

The actual inspected asset is an **8000 × 4500** landscape PNG encoded as RGBA, although every inspected alpha value is fully opaque. It has a 16:9 source canvas with substantial white padding around the central artwork, so it is neither a transparent master nor a tight production crop. Its composition contains:

- a wide, saturated red rectangular field;
- a two-line white uppercase wordmark, with “BLOWIN'” above “SMOKE”;
- extremely heavy, condensed, forward-leaning letterforms;
- a large black-outlined white cloud/speech-volume shape extending beyond the red field;
- high black/white/red contrast and a poster-like silhouette;
- substantial detail and a broad footprint that lose clarity when reduced too far.

The mark communicates confidence, volume, speed, challenge, graphic directness, and a non-corporate point of view. The cloud behaves as a framing device as much as an illustration. The red bar gives the wordmark editorial force; the heavy black contour gives it visual authority.

The logo does **not** establish a universal interface shape, border weight, typeface, or state color. It is a brand artifact, not a component kit.

## 4. Logo Translation Principles

### What the logo may influence

- confident editorial scale and abrupt contrast;
- selective red fields or pressure bars;
- occasional bold condensed or oblique display type;
- cropped rectangular framing in campaign/editorial contexts;
- controlled cloud or speech-volume geometry as a rare frame, mask, or transition;
- occasional heavy black outline in authored graphics—not utilities;
- decisive, short statements with clear hierarchy.

### What the logo must not cause

- every surface becoming red;
- thick black borders around every component;
- a cartoon interface or comic-book component set;
- smoke/cloud illustrations throughout the site;
- every heading imitating the wordmark;
- stretched, cropped, recolored, redrawn, or effect-treated logo artwork;
- loud PDP, proof, compatibility, cart, error, or support surfaces.

**Governing ratio:** the logo's character informs the system more often through contrast, scale, and pacing than through literal repetition of its shapes.

## 5. Three Explored Visual Directions

The directions below are conceptual studies. Only one governs the system.

### Direction A — Pressure & Proof — SELECTED

A white- and near-black commerce field supports accurate product media and compact data. Brand red appears as editorial punctuation, not ambient UI color. Condensed/oblique display moments and rare cloud-derived framing create pressure upstream; downstream decision surfaces use neutral typography, calm dividers, and explicit semantic states.

### Direction B — Redline Utility

An industrial editorial system built around red rails, black rules, dense utilitarian labels, and exposed grid logic. It strongly reflects the wordmark and handles technical information, but repeated red/black structure risks aggression, smoke-shop cliché, and confusion between brand and error states.

### Direction C — Independent Archive

A documentary, maker-led catalog language emphasizing white space, evidence captions, measurement marks, provenance, and monochrome photography. It offers longevity and proof clarity, especially for glass, but under-expresses the challenge, speed, and graphic confidence of the current logo.

### Comparative decision

| CRITERION | PRESSURE & PROOF | REDLINE UTILITY | INDEPENDENT ARCHIVE |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brand fit | High: energy is concentrated and recognizable | High initially; risks caricature | Medium: authentic but too subdued |
| Commerce clarity | High: quiet decision zones are structural | Medium: rails and outlines can compete | High: documentary clarity |
| Originality | High: deliberate intensity gradient | Medium: industrial red/black is familiar | Medium-high: craft/editorial familiar |
| Scalability | High across editorial, technical, proof, cart | Medium in dense states | High, but limited campaign range |
| Accessibility | High when semantic palette remains separate | Medium; red overload creates ambiguity | High |
| Product-range flexibility | High across all divisions | Medium; softer/craft products feel forced | Medium; vape energy may feel muted |
| Longevity | High: expression can evolve over stable grammar | Medium: high-style treatment can date | High but risks blandness |

**Selected direction:** Pressure & Proof best satisfies Article I and the completed systems without producing generic rebellion, fake luxury, or Glossier imitation.

## 6. Visual Constitution

1. Product truth outranks graphic expression.
2. One surface has one dominant visual purpose.
3. White space groups decisions; it is not empty luxury signaling.
4. Brand red signals identity and editorial emphasis, not universal action or status.
5. Near-black carries core reading and primary neutral action.
6. State colors remain semantically independent from the brand palette.
7. Component grammar stays stable across divisions and page families.
8. Density follows consequence and comparison need—not brand mood.
9. Images earn size by answering recognition, scale, connection, contents, craft, or context questions.
10. Motion explains topology, sequence, state, or feedback and provides equivalent reduced motion.
11. Every hover treatment has focus and touch equivalence.
12. Unknown, stale, missing, incompatible, and restricted states are designed as first-class information.
13. Decoration retreats whenever configuration, evidence, or recovery becomes the task.

## 9. Typography Roles

No final licensed font family is approved by this document. The system requires complementary roles, not one wordmark-like voice everywhere.

| ROLE | CHARACTER | USE | CONSTRAINT |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display | Bold, condensed, optionally oblique; high tension | Home propositions, division openings, rare editorial transitions | Short phrases only; never body, data, errors, or every heading; must not imitate logo lettering |
| Headline | Confident, upright, compact but highly legible | Section and page hierarchy across the house | Works across mixed case; quieter than Display |
| Product Identity | Neutral, sturdy, scan-friendly | Product names, model/part identity, maker/object titles | Does not carry specifications or promotional copy |
| Body | Highly readable, open forms, comfortable extended reading | Descriptions, guides, policies, explanations | Never condensed/italic by default; supports zoom and long content |
| Label | Compact, clear, structurally distinct | Fields, options, filters, eyebrows, state labels | Uppercase optional only for short labels; tracking and size remain readable |
| Data / Specification | Precise numerals, stable alignment, clear symbols and units | Price, measurements, potency basis, resistance, batches, specification tables | Tabular numerals where comparison requires; monospace is optional, not an aesthetic default |
| Microcopy | Plain, humane, uncompressed | Help, errors, recovery, consent, captions | Never tiny, low-contrast, slang-heavy, or all caps |

### Hierarchy rules

- Loudness comes from scale, weight, width, and placement—not multiple competing faces.
- Display type can echo the logo's confidence and forward motion without tracing its form.
- Quiet commerce roles remain upright and literal.
- Data values and units stay visually bound; variant and status labels cannot be mistaken for promotion.
- Long labels, technical names, apostrophes, mixed units, and assistive zoom must be tested before fonts are approved.

## 10. Color System

Red, black, and white form the inherited brand core. They are not the entire functional palette.

### Core roles

| ROLE | GOVERNING USE |
|---|---|
| Brand Red | Logo adjacency, house identity, editorial punctuation, active division context, selective curated framing |
| Primary Text | Near-black for identity, body, product truth, and neutral primary action |
| Secondary Text | Dark neutral with sufficient contrast for supporting explanations and metadata |
| Page Background | White or restrained near-white that does not tint product truth |
| Alternate Background | Subtle neutral used to pace sections, education, and comparison—not fake luxury |
| Raised Surface | Visibly distinct neutral surface for drawers/dialogs or necessary topology |
| Border / Divider | Low-emphasis neutral; stronger only for focus, selection, or semantic state |
| Muted Information | Neutral field with readable text; never low-contrast disappearance |

The current raster's red field samples as `#ED2925`; this is an **asset observation, not an approved production token**. The file has no embedded color profile, and final digital/print consistency is unresolved. The sampled red with white normal-sized text requires particular contrast caution. The logo artwork itself is a brand mark; its internal color pairing does not automatically authorize the same pairing for UI copy.

### Semantic roles

| STATE ROLE | VISUAL INTENT | REQUIRED NON-COLOR CUE |
|---|---|---|
| Success | Confirmed completion or valid result | Explicit success label/icon and outcome text |
| Warning | Material caution that may allow progression | Warning label/icon plus consequence |
| Error | Failed action or invalid input | Error label/icon, field linkage, recovery |
| Restricted | Eligibility rule prevents progression | Restricted label, rule category, blocked-action structure |
| Unknown | No reliable determination | Question/unknown symbol, “Unknown/Unverified,” what is missing |
| Informational | Neutral explanation or changed context | Information label/icon and clear scope |
| Proof | Evidence exists with a named status/scope | Document/source/status/date structure |
| Compatible | Verified relationship | “Compatible” text, relationship symbol, applicable objects |
| Incompatible | Verified conflict | “Incompatible” text, blocked relationship, reason |
| Conditional Compatibility | Works only under conditions | “Conditional” text, condition list, intermediary/constraint |

Success/Compatible may share a semantic family only if their labels remain distinct. Error/Incompatible/Restricted require separate meanings even if related hues are used. Unknown is a neutral unresolved state, not a weak success or generic warning.

### Where brand red deliberately disappears

Brand red is normally absent from proof result tables, compatibility matrices, physical-fit checks, eligibility evaluation, technical specifications, cart issue lists, form errors, and support case detail. These use neutral structure plus dedicated semantic roles. Red appears there only if an approved semantic error/restriction color independently requires it—not because the brand owns red.

Neutral or near-black should usually carry the primary commerce action. Red may carry an editorial “enter,” “discover,” or house-level action after contrast validation, but it is not the universal CTA color.

## 11. Spacing and Density

The spacing system is relational. Exact values remain open.

| SCALE ROLE | RELATIONSHIP |
|---|---|
| Intra-control | Tightest; binds icon, label, value, and control state |
| Intra-component | Binds facts that form one decision, such as price and basis |
| Inter-component | Separates adjacent tasks within one module |
| Module | Separates distinct decisions while retaining page continuity |
| Section | Creates editorial pacing and major shifts in purpose |
| House / Campaign | Largest interval; reserved for high-level identity moments |

White space must explain grouping. More space is not automatically more premium; too much distance between a label, state, and consequence damages comprehension.

Density modes:

- **Editorial:** open, asymmetrical pacing with one strong anchor.
- **Discovery:** open orientation plus efficient product groups.
- **Comparison:** compact repeated rows/cards with stable alignment.
- **Decision:** tight dependency clusters separated from secondary detail.
- **Evidence:** compact label/value/source structure with enough breathing room to avoid data blur.
- **Recovery:** compact, direct, and humane; error and action stay together.

---

## 12. Grid and Content Width

The system uses shared alignment anchors rather than one fixed column count.

1. **House frame:** establishes global edge alignment for header, page identity, and major sections.
2. **Editorial field:** permits controlled asymmetry, overlap, cropping, and offset copy on brand/discovery surfaces.
3. **Commerce field:** uses repeatable product-card columns and stable comparison alignment.
4. **Decision field:** binds media, identity, configuration, state, and action; exact arrangement can change responsively without changing semantic order.
5. **Reading measure:** limits long-form guides, policies, explanations, and support prose.
6. **Data measure:** supports tables, fit relationships, proof results, and specifications without forcing them into narrow prose width.

Content width follows content type. Editorial imagery may approach the viewport edge; body text may not. Data views may use more width than prose and may recompose into labeled groups rather than unreadable horizontal compression. Exact container widths, gutters, column counts, and breakpoints require page stress testing.

## 13. Surfaces and Elevation

| SURFACE | PURPOSE | VISUAL RULE |
|---|---|---|
| Page Base | Default reading and commerce field | White/near-white, no decorative shadow |
| Alternate Section | Editorial rhythm or education grouping | Subtle neutral or deliberate brand field upstream only |
| Media Well | Separates product evidence from page | Quiet neutral calibrated to subject; no fake studio effect |
| Data / Evidence Panel | Groups proof, fit, eligibility, or specs | Flat, literal, divider-led; semantic state at edge/header |
| Raised Utility | Search, navigation, quick cart | Clear topology via edge, backdrop, or restrained elevation |
| Dialog | Bounded interrupting decision | Strong focus separation; no ornamental depth |
| Status Field | State plus consequence and recovery | Structure and semantic cue, not a decorative badge |

Elevation communicates topology only. Cards do not float by default. Hover shadows are not required to imply interactivity; focus, cursor, media response, underline, or action change can do that more precisely.

## 14. Borders and Dividers

- Default dividers are quiet and reinforce alignment, comparison, or grouping.
- Strong borders identify selected controls, focus, or high-priority semantic states.
- Black outline language derived from the logo is reserved for editorial graphics and rare house-level framing.
- Component outlines do not stack around surface, card, input, badge, and inner panel simultaneously.
- Dashed or specialized rules may distinguish provisional/unknown data only after comprehension testing; text remains authoritative.
- Dividers must not create spreadsheet noise on otherwise simple pages.

---

## 15. Radius

Radius communicates component family and touch affordance, not friendliness as decoration.

- Use a restrained small family for inputs, buttons, cards, media, drawers, and panels.
- Editorial frames may use sharp or intentionally irregular crop geometry independent of component radius.
- Pills are reserved for compact binary/short state or filter controls where the shape improves comprehension; not every label or button becomes a pill.
- Proof, technical data, and cart panels should not become soft bubbly containers.
- Final radius values require cross-component and logo-adjacency testing.

---

## 16. Iconography

The icon system is functional, direct, and consistent in construction. It supports search, account, cart, navigation, media controls, disclosure, status, measurement, connection, document, and support tasks.

- Ambiguous icons receive visible or accessible labels.
- State icons are paired with text and never reuse one glyph for incompatible meanings.
- Connection, direction, and relationship diagrams use arrows/lines only when the endpoints and state are labeled.
- Decorative smoke clouds are not utility icons, loaders, notification shapes, or error symbols.
- A final library may be licensed, adapted under permission, or custom-built; mixing unrelated sets is prohibited.

## 17. Product Imagery

Product imagery is evidence before it is atmosphere. Every image slot must have a declared job, and no image may imply a property that the catalog cannot substantiate.

| IMAGE ROLE | REQUIRED COMMUNICATION | VISUAL DISCIPLINE |
|---|---|---|
| Primary identity | What the exact product or selected variant is | Clean silhouette, faithful color, stable crop family |
| Alternate angle | Form that the primary view cannot show | Preserve orientation; avoid arbitrary editorial rotation |
| Variant evidence | The visible difference between selectable variants | Match option names and selection state exactly |
| Scale | How large the object is in a decision-useful frame | Use standardized reference or labeled measurement, not forced perspective |
| Connection / fit | Where and how components meet | Show endpoints, direction, and conditional requirements |
| Included contents | What arrives in the package | Separate included items from compatible or optional items |
| Assembly / setup | Sequence or configured state | Number steps; pair with text; never make animation the only explanation |
| Material / craft | Surface, finish, workmanship, or maker evidence | Close detail with honest lighting and no defect-concealing treatment |
| Use context | Operating posture or environmental scale | Show lawful, responsible context without inventing performance claims |

Governing rules:

- Neutral product views use repeatable lighting, perspective, background behavior, and subject scale within a category.
- Product truth must remain legible without enlarging an art-directed photograph.
- Critical dimensions, compatibility, contents, eligibility, safety, and availability are also expressed as structured text.
- Responsive crops are chosen by image role. The system must not blindly center-crop away a mouthpiece, connector, joint, control, label, or measurement reference.
- Image galleries expose media type and position, support keyboard and touch navigation, and retain the selected variant's truth.
- Zoom is evidence-oriented: it should reveal detail without substituting a different image or variant.
- Missing media uses an explicit unavailable-image state; it never substitutes a similar product.
- Lifestyle media cannot occupy the sole primary evidence position on a consequential PDP.

## 18. Editorial Imagery

Editorial imagery carries independence, human presence, and discovery. It must feel observed and specific rather than stock, aspirational-by-default, or cosmetically perfected.

- Favor real operators, makers, customers, places, materials, and moments with permission and context.
- Direct for conviction and activity: people doing, building, choosing, maintaining, or explaining—not posing as generic “culture.”
- Contrast, crop, movement, and imperfect environmental texture may create energy upstream in the journey.
- Preserve skin, material, smoke/vapor, glass, packaging, and location honestly. Atmospheric treatment cannot obscure a product, normalize unsafe behavior, or imply an unverified effect.
- Use captions or adjoining copy when provenance materially strengthens the story.
- Avoid cannabis-leaf wallpaper, anonymous smoke clouds, neon “head-shop” clichés, staged rebellion, beauty-editorial imitation, and borrowed street signifiers.
- Editorial images may be visually assertive on home, division, story, and guide openings. As the interface approaches selection, eligibility, fit, price, or cart commitment, product evidence replaces atmosphere.

## 19. Graphic Devices

The visual language may derive original devices from the logo's observable behavior—pressure, volume, overlap, outline, and red/black/white contrast—without duplicating the logo illustration throughout the interface.

Approved device families:

- **Pressure field:** a bounded red or dark field behind a short, declarative editorial message.
- **Cropped volume:** a large shape or type fragment that enters/exits a composition to create tension at section scale.
- **Witness rule:** a strong line that anchors a claim to proof, source, measurement, or next action.
- **Connection notation:** labeled lines and endpoints for physical or platform relationships.
- **Measure notation:** restrained dimension markers tied to verified values.
- **Challenge lockup:** the approved “Who Wants That Smoke?” expression used at selected brand moments, never as constant UI decoration.

Frequency is hierarchical. A page normally has one leading graphic gesture, a small number of supporting rules/notations, and many quiet components. Devices never sit behind body copy, imitate a status, reduce contrast, or occupy decision controls. Decorative smoke is not a system primitive.

## 20. Product-Card System

All product cards share one semantic grammar while permitting division-specific evidence. Their job is recognition, comparison, and a truthful next step—not to compress a PDP into a tile.

### 20.1 Canonical anatomy

1. Media well with exact product/variant image and meaningful alt behavior.
2. Division label when the surrounding context is mixed.
3. Maker/brand when it materially aids recognition.
4. Product name as the strongest text identity.
5. Product role or type in customer language.
6. Two or three category-relevant comparison facts.
7. Material proof, compatibility, physical-fit, or eligibility state in its governed state anatomy—not as a promotional badge.
8. Price presentation, including range or original/current relationship when truthful.
9. One controlled promotional state, if warranted.
10. Availability or fulfillment consequence.
11. One primary next step appropriate to readiness.

### 20.2 Visual and interaction rules

- Cards are alignment-led and low-chrome; a border and shadow are not the default container.
- Product name, price, state, and action keep stable positions within a collection context so scanning does not become a hunt.
- Media is prominent but cannot crowd out a blocker. State and consequence may outrank imagery.
- One promotional claim is the maximum at card level. Operational, proof, eligibility, compatibility, and fit states are not badges. Their visual priority may rise with consequence, and promotions never displace them.
- Hover may reveal a verified alternate view or clarify the action, but the default state remains complete and touch users lose nothing.
- Focus is conspicuous and follows the same semantic boundary as the clickable area. Nested controls have independent names and focus.
- Variant swatches/options appear only when their state, name, and image can remain synchronized. A count/link is preferable to a misleading miniature selector.
- Quick add is permitted only when no unresolved option, compatibility, eligibility, or consequential configuration remains.
- Loading, added, failed, sold-out, restricted, and price-change states retain card geometry where practical and announce meaningful changes.

### 20.3 Division evidence slots

| DIVISION | HIGH-VALUE CARD FACTS | FACTS THAT REQUIRE DEEPER CONTEXT |
|---|---|---|
| THCA | two or three as relevant: format; quantity/strain context; one properly scoped composition/potency fact; applicable proof or eligibility signal | full laboratory evidence/results, methods, complete composition, policy detail |
| Vape & Nicotine | product role, platform/connection, quantity, compatible/unknown cue | full device matrix, configuration dependencies, replacement instructions |
| Glass & Accessories | object role, principal joint/fit fact, principal dimension/material | complete measurements, clearance/orientation conditions, care and fragile-shipping detail |

## 21. Buttons and Actions

Action hierarchy communicates consequence, not brand enthusiasm.

| ACTION ROLE | VISUAL TREATMENT | USE |
|---|---|---|
| Primary commerce | Highest neutral contrast, solid, stable | The single purchase-readiness action in a decision region |
| Primary editorial | May use the controlled brand field or strong text treatment | Enter a division, story, guide, or campaign |
| Secondary | Quieter outline or lower-emphasis solid | Alternative route without equal consequence |
| Tertiary | Text or icon-plus-text with clear affordance | Details, compare, edit, remove, disclose |
| Destructive | Separate semantic treatment and confirmation when needed | Remove, clear, cancel irreversible work |
| State / blocked | State-specific label plus reason and recovery | Unavailable, restricted, incompatible, unknown, incomplete selection |

- A bounded region has one dominant action.
- Labels name the result: “Choose options,” “Check compatibility,” “View proof,” or “Add to cart” rather than ambiguous enthusiasm.
- Red is not automatically the purchase button. Primary commerce should normally use an accessible neutral high-contrast treatment so brand red does not compete with errors, restrictions, or editorial emphasis.
- Disabled styling never carries the explanation alone. Place the reason near the action and provide the recovery path when one exists.
- Processing retains the action width, prevents duplicate submission, communicates progress in text/nonvisual state, and resolves to success or actionable failure.
- Targets, focus rings, label contrast, and pressed/selected distinction meet accessibility requirements at every responsive size.

## 22. Forms and Option Selectors

Forms are calm, explicit, and persistent. They do not rely on placeholder text, color, or visual memory.

- Every control has a programmatic and visible label except where an established icon control has an unambiguous accessible name.
- Help text precedes failure when a format, legal condition, or compatibility dependency is predictable.
- Validation appears near the field and in a navigable summary for multi-field failures.
- Required/optional status is stated consistently; disabled fields explain why if customers could reasonably expect access.
- Autofill, keyboard, screen-reader, zoom, high-contrast, and touch operation are first-class states.
- Selectors show **default/unselected, hover, focus, selected, unavailable, incompatible, conditionally available, unknown, error, and loading** as applicable.
- Text labels remain authoritative. Color/image swatches include names, availability, and selected state; visually similar choices are never color-only.
- Option groups expose the decision dimension (“Size,” “Resistance,” “Joint size,” “Pack amount”) and update price, image, availability, proof, fit, and action state together.
- A selection that changes the exact SKU or relationship must visibly update the product identity context; silent substitution is prohibited.

## 23. Global Navigation Visual System

The global shell is a quiet orientation instrument around an expressive brand, not an always-on campaign.

- The approved full logo may anchor spacious brand contexts; a future compact approved mark is required for persistent headers and small screens.
- Primary navigation exposes the three divisions as peers within one house. The active division is explicit through text, position, and a restrained non-color-dependent cue.
- Utility navigation—search, account, location/fulfillment when applicable, and cart—uses one consistent icon/label grammar.
- Announcement space is reserved for genuinely useful operational, eligibility, shipping, service, or campaign information. It is dismissible when persistent value does not justify permanent height.
- Mega-menu and mobile navigation organize by customer task and product role, not by the visual need to fill columns.
- Overlays use clear topology, predictable dismissal, trapped/returned focus when modal, and no decorative competition with their choices.
- Scroll compaction may reduce shell height but cannot remove essential orientation, search access, cart access, or a readable brand identifier.
- Search is presented as a primary discovery route, not a tiny utility. Suggestions distinguish products, categories, guides, makers/brands, and verified relationship queries.
- Navigation red is a measured orientation/accent signal; it does not wash the persistent shell.

## 24. Product-Detail Hierarchy

The universal PDP moves from identification to configuration to verification to commitment.

1. **Identity:** division/context, maker/brand, exact product name, role/type, current variant/SKU.
2. **Primary evidence:** truthful media matched to the selected state.
3. **Required decisions:** options and configuration in dependency order.
4. **Commercial facts:** price, quantity/amount, availability, fulfillment consequence.
5. **Material decision states and requirements:** eligibility, proof, compatibility, physical fit, included/required components, conditions, unknowns, and blockers applicable to the exact selection.
6. **Primary action:** state-aware result and reason after the material states above have been composed.
7. **Full evidence and specifications:** deeper source, scope, methods, measurements, contents, conditions, and last-verified context.
8. **Use, care, setup, and replacement:** appropriate lifecycle guidance.
9. **Verified relationships:** compatible complements, replacements, alternatives, or bundles with relationship labels.
10. **Policies and support:** shipping, returns, fragile handling, age/legal requirements, contact route.

Visual rules:

- Media and purchase controls may share the opening viewport, but neither may cause blockers to disappear below a promotional module.
- The purchase panel is quiet: limited brand red, no atmospheric graphic layer, no carousel movement, and no recommendation intrusion.
- Related evidence is grouped by decision, not hidden merely to shorten the page. A collapsed summary still exposes its status.
- Sticky purchase affordances may preserve a valid selected state; they cannot conceal changed price, eligibility, compatibility, availability, or required options.
- The customer should always be able to answer: **What is this? Which exact one am I considering? Will it work for me? Can I buy it? What will happen next?**

## 25. Category and Search Hierarchy

Category and search pages are comparison environments. Their visual quality comes from ordered density, not maximal emptiness.

- A compact title/context region states the scope, result meaning, and any legally or operationally important boundary.
- Filters are grouped by decision dimension, use human labels, expose applied state, and distinguish zero results, unavailable inventory, and unknown data.
- Sort labels describe the ordering consequence; merchandising order is not disguised as objective relevance.
- Result count and active constraints remain visible enough to explain why the set changed.
- Cards align on comparison-critical fields while allowing variable names and governed state messages.
- Search query interpretation, corrections, and category/guide suggestions are explicit; the system never silently changes the customer's term.
- No-results states preserve the query and filters, explain the condition, and offer widening, correction, education, or support—never a generic dead end.
- Mobile filtering uses a clear applied-count summary and returns focus/state to the result context. Desktop density must not be reproduced as squeezed columns.
- Red may identify active context or one editorial interruption; it does not color every filter, badge, price, and action.

## 26. Proof, Compatibility, Fit, Eligibility, and Unknown States

These are decision systems, not decorative badges. They share a visual anatomy while keeping their meanings distinct.

### 26.1 State anatomy

Every material state exposes, as available:

1. **State label** in plain language.
2. **Subject and scope**—which SKU, selection, destination, device, component, batch, or relationship is evaluated.
3. **Customer consequence**—what can or cannot happen.
4. **Conditions or missing inputs.**
5. **Evidence/source and verification context** when relevant.
6. **Recovery or next action.**

Text is authoritative. Color, icon, edge treatment, and position provide redundant cues. Unknown/unverified is a first-class state and never visually collapses into compatible, eligible, passed, or absent.

### 26.2 Semantic distinction

| SYSTEM | CUSTOMER QUESTION | VISUAL REQUIREMENT |
|---|---|---|
| Product proof | “What substantiates this product fact?” | Source/scope/date linkage; quiet documentary treatment |
| Compatibility | “Will these platform or component roles work together?” | Relationship endpoints, state, conditions, reverse route |
| Physical fit | “Will these exact forms and measurements connect and clear?” | Labeled dimensions/orientation, conditional geometry, verified pair where required |
| Eligibility | “May this customer receive this item in this context?” | Jurisdiction/age/fulfillment scope, blocker timing, lawful recovery only |
| Unknown / unverified | “What has not been established?” | Visibly incomplete state, reason/source gap, no affirmative styling |

Eligibility is not compatibility. Availability is not eligibility. A related item is not necessarily compatible. Proof is not a marketing seal. The interface must not let shared color or badge geometry blur these distinctions.

## 27. Quick Cart and Full Cart

### 27.1 Quick cart

The quick cart confirms a recent action and supports a fast, reversible review. It is a quiet raised utility surface with exact line identity, selected options, quantity, price, availability/eligibility/relationship changes, subtotal context, and a clear path to the full cart or back to shopping. Checkout readiness is determined on the revalidated full cart, not in this drawer.

- Opening does not erase page or selection context; closing returns focus to the initiating control.
- Arbitrary cross-sells are prohibited. The only recommendation-like route is a concise missing-required-component path when that requirement and relationship are verified; it never appears ahead of a blocker or price consequence.
- Success motion may confirm placement but cannot replace text or persistently occupy attention.
- Any failure leaves the source item and choices intact and names a recovery.

### 27.2 Full cart

The full cart is a consequence ledger. Visual brand intensity drops to its minimum practical level.

- Each line exposes exact product/variant, required relationship context, unit and extended price, quantity, fulfillment/availability, and editable decisions.
- Compatibility, fit, eligibility, inventory, price, and fulfillment are revalidated and surfaced at the affected line plus an order-level summary when necessary.
- Unknown relationships remain unknown. The system cannot use a green/general-success cart state to imply that unverified components work together.
- Promotions follow blockers and totals; they do not interrupt the correction path.
- Order summary makes merchandise, discounts, estimated/known charges, and uncertainty legible without visual tricks.
- Checkout action is dominant only when readiness is truthful. Blocked states explain exactly what must change.

## 28. Education and Guide Visual System

Education earns trust by making the decision easier, not by disguising promotion as instruction.

- Guide openings may use the fullest editorial type, image, and graphic-device expression after the home and division landings.
- Body content uses a controlled reading measure, strong heading landmarks, descriptive links, and sufficient paragraph/list separation.
- Definitions, warnings, steps, comparison tables, diagrams, citations, and update context each have stable visual roles.
- Decision tools may bridge from learning to commerce, but product modules are labeled and visually subordinate to the educational claim they support.
- Technical diagrams identify parts, direction, measurement, or lifecycle with a legend; decoration never resembles evidence.
- Long pages expose progress through headings, table of contents, or local navigation without forcing scroll effects.
- Author/reviewer, source, and reviewed/updated context appear when the topic affects safety, legality, eligibility, compatibility, fit, or product truth.
- Division education uses the shared house grammar. It differs through subject matter and evidence—not separate microsite styling.

## 29. Operational, Empty, Error, and Missing-Data States

Operational states are part of the premium system, not leftovers. They remain literal, stable, and helpful under pressure.

| STATE | REQUIRED CONTENT | VISUAL BEHAVIOR |
|---|---|---|
| Loading | What region is updating, when known | Preserve layout; avoid full-page interruption for local work |
| Empty | What is empty and why that may be expected | Keep context and offer relevant next action |
| No results | Query/constraints and widening routes | Preserve entered work; distinguish from system error |
| Error | What failed, what remains safe, what to try | Strong semantic cue, focus/announcement, no blame |
| Offline / timeout | Connection consequence and retained state | Allow retry; do not imply transaction success |
| Unavailable | Exact item/variant and inventory consequence | Retain identity; offer verified alternatives or notification if supported |
| Restricted / ineligible | Scope and lawful reason/recovery | No teasing disabled commerce; separate from stock state |
| Stale / changed | What changed since prior view | Require acknowledgement when consequence is material |
| Missing data | Which fact is absent and its effect | Render UNKNOWN / UNVERIFIED; never omit the row to imply normality |
| Media unavailable | Exact media gap | Neutral placeholder with product name/role; never substitute a neighboring SKU |

Skeletons, spinners, and progress indicators use restrained neutral motion. Error pages and empty carts do not use cute smoke mascots or jokey copy when money, eligibility, or product usability is involved.

## 30. Motion System

Motion explains interface state and spatial relationship. It does not manufacture premium feeling through delay.

### 30.1 Motion roles

| ROLE | PURPOSE | EXAMPLES |
|---|---|---|
| Topology | Show where a layer came from and where it returns | Navigation panel, quick cart, search, modal |
| Selection | Confirm a customer-controlled choice | Option, filter, media, tab, accordion |
| Feedback | Confirm success, failure, or changed state | Added item, saved preference, validation |
| Progress | Explain ongoing work | Search update, inventory check, submission |
| Media control | Allow deliberate inspection | Gallery transition, zoom, product rotation if evidence-justified |
| Editorial emphasis | Support one upstream narrative moment | Hero/section reveal under strict restraint |

### 30.2 Governing behavior

- Customer input causes motion; ambient loops and auto-advancing commerce content are disfavored.
- Duration and easing values are deliberately not specified here. They require prototype, device, and accessibility testing.
- State changes begin promptly, travel the shortest comprehensible path, and never delay access to content or action.
- Motion preserves semantic continuity: the selected product, variant, price, image, and action cannot appear to detach from one another.
- Scroll-linked effects cannot gate information, hijack scrolling, or move decision content unexpectedly.
- Hover-only media transitions have an equivalent static/touch route.
- Success effects are brief and quiet. Error and restriction states do not shake, flash, or punish the customer.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` and an equivalent product-level motion-control strategy remove nonessential translation, parallax, auto-motion, and large zoom while retaining immediate state communication.
- Pause/stop controls are required for any moving content that meets applicable accessibility thresholds. Essential motion must have a non-motion equivalent wherever possible.

## 31. Accessibility Requirements

Accessibility is a governing visual constraint across all themes, states, divisions, and responsive layouts.

- Text and meaningful graphics meet applicable WCAG contrast requirements against every actual surface; logos are not used as precedent for interface-text contrast.
- Color is never the only carrier of status, selection, requiredness, incompatibility, restriction, proof, success, or error.
- Visible keyboard focus is high-contrast, unobscured, and not confused with selected state.
- Reading and interaction order follows visual order; responsive reflow does not create a different semantic sequence.
- Text supports zoom and user spacing without clipping, collision, or loss of action/state labels.
- Touch targets and spacing support coarse pointers without forcing oversized desktop controls.
- Images have role-appropriate alternatives: informative alt text, empty alternatives for redundant decoration, and adjacent structured data for complex evidence.
- Icons and swatches have accessible names. Tooltips cannot contain essential information available only on hover.
- Dialogs, drawers, menus, galleries, and disclosures manage focus, announce state, and restore context.
- Validation identifies the field, error, and resolution. Status updates use appropriate live-region behavior without repeated interruption.
- Tables preserve headings/relationships; on small screens they reformat without losing comparison meaning.
- Media includes controls, captions/transcripts where required, no unexpected audio, and reduced-motion behavior.
- Brand type, uppercase, oblique forms, and compressed display styles are never used where they compromise essential reading.
- The system is tested with keyboard-only use, screen readers, zoom/reflow, forced/high-contrast modes, reduced motion, touch, and representative low-vision/color-vision conditions.

## 32. Responsive Visual Priority

Responsive design preserves decision priority rather than shrinking a desktop composition.

### 32.1 Priority order

1. Identity and current selection.
2. Blocker or consequential state.
3. Required choices in dependency order.
4. Price, availability, and fulfillment consequence.
5. Material proof, compatibility, fit, eligibility, contents, and requirements for the exact selection.
6. The state-derived primary action.
7. Full specifications, methods, helpful education, and verified relationships.
8. Editorial expression and secondary promotion.

### 32.2 Rules

- The smallest layout is composed around a single primary reading/action column; wide layouts add adjacency, not a new information hierarchy.
- Side-by-side PDP media and purchase regions stack in the same decision sequence when width is constrained.
- Sticky actions include enough selected-state and consequence context to be trustworthy, or they are omitted.
- Comparison tables may recompose to labeled records; hidden columns cannot remove a blocker or make products appear equivalent.
- Filters move into a controlled layer with visible applied state; navigation changes mode without changing vocabulary.
- Editorial crops change intentionally by breakpoint family and protect subject, logo clear space, captions, and overlay contrast.
- Card grids change column count and density while card anatomy and fact priority remain stable.
- Very wide screens preserve readable/content measures rather than stretching prose, purchase controls, or product images indefinitely.
- Exact breakpoints remain open and must be derived from content stress tests, not copied from a reference site or device list.

| Novelty glassmorphism | Reduces legibility and creates decorative surface complexity |
| Badge piles | Forces promotions, states, and proof into visual competition without priority |
| Fake luxury minimalism | Uses emptiness, tiny type, and obscurity as status while withholding useful truth |
| Three unrelated division themes | Fragments one brand, teaches three interaction grammars, and corrupts semantic color |
| Generic SaaS styling | Makes physical products and culture feel administratively abstract |
| Tiny technical text | Hides the information most necessary to prevent unusable or ineligible purchases |
| Brand graphics competing with product truth | Reverses the central quieting principle at the moment trust matters most |
| Motion that delays decisions | Manufactures ceremony at the expense of control, speed, and accessibility |
| Wordmark imitation across headings | Converts a logo characteristic into an illegible typographic monoculture |
| Beauty-derived palette, photography, proportions, or card styling | Reproduces Glossier-specific expression rather than approved UX discipline |
| Flavor/effect/material encoded only by color or image | Creates ambiguity and inaccessible decision states |
| Compatibility or proof treated as generic “verified” decoration | Conceals subject, scope, conditions, evidence, and unknowns |
| Promotion ahead of restriction, incompatibility, or price consequence | Manipulates attention and can produce a legitimate but unusable purchase |

## 39. Next Phase Recommendation

**Selected next phase: A. HIGH-FIDELITY PAGE DESIGN SPECIFICATIONS.**

The governing direction is chosen, but exact tokens should not be frozen in abstraction. High-fidelity specifications for a deliberately small, representative set of pages can test the system at its extremes: expressive home/division entry, comparison-dense category/search, a quiet universal PDP plus domain-specialized PDP states, and full-cart consequence handling. That work will expose the content, state, responsive, accessibility, and asset pressures needed to resolve fonts, spacing, widths, color, card geometry, diagrams, and motion responsibly.

This recommendation does not begin that phase and does not grant implementation authorization. Content/voice, technical architecture, and formal token publication remain necessary, but high-fidelity visual stress testing should precede final visual token values.
